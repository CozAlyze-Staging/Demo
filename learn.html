The Basics

Birth Chart

›

A birth chart is a map of the sky at the moment and place you were born. It shows where the Sun, the Moon and the planets were, which sign each one was in, and which sign was rising in the east.

Every reading on CozAlyze starts from this map. In Vedic astrology the main birth chart is called the Rashi chart, or kundli.

Zodiac Signs

›

The twelve signs, Aries through Pisces, divide the circle of the sky into twelve equal parts of 30 degrees each. Each sign has its own character, and a planet takes on some of that character from the sign it sits in.

Vedic and Western astrology use the same twelve signs but start counting from different points, which is why your signs can differ between the two.

Planets

›

Each planet stands for a different part of life:

  • Sun: identity, vitality and purpose
  • Moon: the mind, feelings and what you need to feel settled
  • Mercury: thinking, learning and speech
  • Venus: love, beauty and pleasure
  • Mars: drive, courage and action
  • Jupiter: growth, wisdom and good fortune
  • Saturn: discipline, effort and time

Vedic astrology calls them grahas and works with nine: these seven plus Rahu and Ketu. Western astrology also uses Uranus, Neptune and Pluto.

Houses

›

The houses divide your chart into twelve areas of life, such as self, money, home, children, work and relationships. A planet’s house shows which part of life it tends to act on.

Your Vedic chart uses whole sign houses, where each sign is one complete house. Your Western chart uses the Placidus system, which divides the sky by time, so houses can be different sizes. For births far enough north or south that Placidus can’t be calculated, CozAlyze uses whole sign houses instead.

Ascendant (Rising Sign)

›

Your Ascendant, or rising sign, is the sign that was coming up over the eastern horizon when you were born. It sets your first house and the order of every house after it, so it shapes how your whole chart reads.

It often describes how you meet the world and how others first see you. In Vedic astrology it’s called the Lagna and is the starting point of the chart.

The rising sign changes about every two hours on average, faster for some signs and slower for others, so it depends on an accurate birth time.

Guiding Planet (Chart Ruler)

›

Every sign is ruled by a planet. The planet that rules your rising sign is your guiding planet, also called the chart ruler, or in Vedic astrology the Lagna lord. Its sign and house show where the energy of your whole chart tends to go.

CozAlyze uses the traditional rulers in both systems, so Scorpio is ruled by Mars, Aquarius by Saturn and Pisces by Jupiter.

Moon Sign

›

Your Moon sign is the sign the Moon was in when you were born. Vedic astrology gives the Moon great weight, because it describes the mind and emotional life: how you feel, how you react, and what helps you feel at peace.

Your Moon is also where your nakshatra and your life cycle timing begin.

Nakshatra

›

Nakshatras are 27 star groups along the Moon’s path, each covering 13 degrees 20 minutes of the zodiac. They are one of the oldest layers of Indian astrology. Each has its own symbol, ruling deity and ruling planet.

Every planet sits in a nakshatra, but the Moon’s, called your birth star, matters most. It colors your emotional nature and sets the timing of your life’s chapters.

Pada

›

Each nakshatra is split into four quarters called padas, each 3 degrees 20 minutes wide, which makes 108 in all. The pada fine-tunes the nakshatra’s meaning.

Each pada also matches a sign in the navamsa, a divisional chart Vedic astrologers use to look more deeply at a planet’s strength and at marriage.

Rahu and Ketu

›

Rahu and Ketu aren’t physical bodies. They are the two points where the Moon’s path crosses the Sun’s path, the places where eclipses happen. Rahu is the north node and Ketu the south node, and they always sit exactly opposite each other.

Vedic astrology counts them as two of the nine grahas. Rahu is traditionally linked with desire, ambition and the unfamiliar, and Ketu with detachment, spiritual insight and letting go. Western astrology reads them as the North Node and South Node.

Aspects

›

An aspect is an angle between two planets, such as a conjunction (together), a square (90 degrees apart) or a trine (120 degrees apart). Aspects show where planets support each other and where they pull against each other.

Vedic astrology uses its own system, called drishti, where each planet looks at certain houses counted from where it sits. Every planet looks at the house opposite, and Mars, Jupiter and Saturn have extra ones of their own.

Transits

›

Transits are where the planets are now, compared with where they were when you were born. As a planet moves through a part of your chart, it can bring its themes into that area of life for a while.

Mahadasha and Antardasha

›

Vedic astrology divides life into chapters ruled by the planets, called dashas. CozAlyze uses Vimshottari, the most widely used system, a 120-year cycle shared among the nine grahas.

Each main period, the Mahadasha, lasts between 6 and 20 years, and each is split into shorter sub-periods called Antardashas. The planet ruling your current period tends to color that chapter of your life. Where your cycle starts comes from your Moon’s nakshatra at birth.

Panchanga

›

Panchanga means “five limbs.” It describes the quality of the day you were born through five factors:

  • Vara: the weekday
  • Tithi: the lunar day
  • Nakshatra: the Moon’s star group
  • Yoga: a pattern formed by the Sun and Moon together
  • Karana: half of a lunar day

You’ll find it on your Vedic birth chart.

Ayanamsa

›

The ayanamsa is the gap between the Western and Vedic zodiacs, now about 24 degrees. CozAlyze uses the Lahiri ayanamsa, the standard set for India’s national calendar and the one most Vedic astrologers use.

Vedic and Western

Two Ways to Measure the Sky

›

Western astrology uses the tropical zodiac, which is tied to the seasons. Aries always begins at the spring equinox.

Vedic astrology uses the sidereal zodiac, which is tied to the stars themselves. The Earth slowly wobbles on its axis, so the equinox slides back against the stars by about one degree every 72 years. Over the centuries the two zodiacs have drifted apart, and today they sit about 24 degrees from each other.

Why Your Signs Can Differ

›

Because of that gap, a planet usually lands one sign earlier in your Vedic chart than in your Western chart. Someone with a Western Sun in Leo will often have a Vedic Sun in Cancer.

A planet keeps the same sign in both only when it sits in roughly the last 6 degrees of its Western sign. Both answers are correct. They’re measured from different starting points.

What Each Lens Shows

›

On CozAlyze, your Western reading looks at how you meet the world: your personality, how you express yourself and how you move through daily life.

Your Vedic reading looks at what lies underneath: your mind and emotional nature, your sense of purpose, and the timing of your life’s chapters. Together they give two views of the same person.

Different Tools

›

Vedic astrology adds the nakshatras, the Panchanga and dasha timing. It works with nine grahas and whole sign houses.

Western astrology adds Uranus, Neptune and Pluto, uses Placidus houses, and leans on the aspects between planets.

Common Questions

Why is my sign different from my horoscope app?

›

Most horoscope apps use the Western zodiac and look only at your Sun sign. Your Vedic chart is measured against the stars, about 24 degrees from the Western zodiac, so your Vedic signs are often one sign earlier. Your Western reading on CozAlyze should match the Sun sign you already know.

Why does my birth time matter so much?

›

The rising sign and the houses turn with the Earth, changing sign about every two hours. The Moon moves quickly too, changing nakshatra about once a day, and the Moon’s exact position sets your dasha dates. A birth certificate or hospital record gives the most reliable time.

What if I don’t know my birth time?

›

Enter your best estimate. Most planets will still be in the right signs, but your rising sign, your houses and your dasha dates may not be correct. On a day when the Moon changes sign or nakshatra, those can be off as well.

Who writes the readings?

›

Your chart is calculated first. The readings are then written by AI from your calculated chart, following CozAlyze’s interpretation rules. Each one is an AI-generated personalized astrology reading, for entertainment purposes only.

How is my chart calculated?

›

Planet positions come from Astronomy Engine, a precise astronomy library. Your Vedic chart uses the Lahiri ayanamsa, the mean lunar node and whole sign houses. Your Western chart uses the tropical zodiac and Placidus houses.

Why are my period dates different from other Vedic software?

›

CozAlyze measures Vimshottari periods in lunar years of 354 days. Most software uses the 365-day solar year. The order and length of the periods are the same, but the dates shift, and the gap grows the older the chart is. They’re two different conventions, not an error in either one.

Does my chart predict what will happen?

›

Your readings describe tendencies and possibilities, not fixed outcomes. Your chart shows patterns you may notice in yourself, and your choices decide what you make of them.

Will my reading change if I open it again?

›

No. A saved reading opens exactly as it was written. Pages about the current sky, like transits and timing, show the date they were calculated for.

Can I print or save my reading?

›

Yes. Your saved readings have a Print / Save PDF button at the top, and Print Your Chart at the end of each birth chart opens the full reading ready to print.
